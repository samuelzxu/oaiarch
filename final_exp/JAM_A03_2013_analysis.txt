1. Historical and Geographical Context  
   - This part of the Jamari interfluve lies within seasonally inundated terrain between tributaries of the Machado (Ji‐Paraná) River. Ethnohistoric and archaeological surveys in nearby sectors of the Floresta Nacional do Jamari have documented sophisticated pre‐Columbian earth‐management systems—raised fields, canals and causeways—used to modulate water in wet–dry cycles.  
   - Radiocarbon dates from analogous sites range ca. 500 – 1500 CE. The orientation and scale of the embankment network fit regional variants of the so‐called “Amazonian garden cities,” where low‐magnitude elevation changes created cultivable plots and drainage.  

2. Integration with Current Theories  
   - The discovery of rectilinear raised‐field cells in this hitherto understudied sector challenges the view that pre‐Columbian engineering in the Jamari forest was sparse and localized. Instead it supports models of region‐wide, coordinated landscape modification.  
   - The existence of smaller mound‐and‐ditch features (anomaly_2) indicates possible habitation or ritual focal points associated with the agricultural grid, consistent with ethnographic analogies of village plazas embedded in field systems.  

3. Advancement of the Discipline  
   - These finds expand the known footprint of earth‐architecture in the southern Amazon, contributing to debates on societal complexity and landscape planning before European contact.  
   - They underscore the need to integrate high‐resolution LiDAR DTM derivatives with spectral data to detect low‐relief archaeology under closed canopy.
