In the Oriximiná region, pre‐Columbian communities built rectilinear networks of causeways, canals, and raised fields between ca. 1200–1650 CE to regulate seasonal flooding and enhance soil fertility (terra preta). Most previous surveys concentrated near major floodplains; the discovery of similar engineered features beneath closed canopy here suggests that sophisticated hydrological planning extended deep into interfluves. If confirmed by ground‐truthing, these anomalies would expand our understanding of Amazonian landscape engineering, showing that upland soils were intensively managed far from navigable rivers and that settlement patterns may have been more dispersed and interconnected than assumed.
