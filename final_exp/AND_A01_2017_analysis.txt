Contextual Interpretation and Implications:
- These features fall within the interfluve landscape where pre‐Columbian societies engineered upland raised fields and canals to buffer flood seasonality and improve soil fertility. Comparable systems have been documented in Bolivia and Peru, but in Brazil they remain under‐reported.
- The large embanked platform (anomaly_1) aligns with patterns of Amazonian dark earth (‘terra preta’) field systems described by Roosevelt (1991) and Heckenberger et al. (2003). The combination of rectilinear earthworks with moisture‐retaining soils suggests intentional water‐management agriculture rather than purely habitation mounds.
- The sinuous canal (anomaly_2) challenges the model that upland interfluve engineering was limited to orthogonal grids. Instead, it implies flexible routing to intercept sub‐surface groundwater or divert ephemeral streams into raised plots.
- The small enclosure (anomaly_3) may represent localized experimentation with plot sizes or crop‐rotation blocks, aligning with ethnographic analogs of manioc cultivation on raised beds.

Broader Theoretical Impact:
These mapped anomalies extend the geographic range of engineered Amazonian upland systems into eastern Pará, suggesting a more continuous zone of landscape modification than previously hypothesized. They support a model of dispersed but technologically sophisticated polities capable of coordinated earthwork construction.
