1. Geographic & Historical Context  
The Jamari interfluve (Floresta Nacional do Jamari, near RO-452 km 7.5) lies within a broader upland zone where Late Holocene societies engineered ridge-and-canal networks to manage water in seasonally wet tropical forests. Comparable systems have been documented along the Purus and Madeira, but this forested upland ridge network has not previously been mapped at this scale in Jamari.

2. Fit with Regional Patterns  
The orthogonal intersection of a primary N–S canal (anomaly_1) with an E–W cross-canal (anomaly_2) mirrors other Amazonian water-management grids. The depressed NIR reflectance along these alignments matches patterns seen in anthropogenic dark earths (terra preta), implying soil enrichment and prolonged use.

3. Theoretical Implications  
Discovery of a multi-kilometer canal/causeway network beneath closed canopy challenges the notion that upland interfluves were marginal to pre-Columbian hydraulic planning. It supports emerging models of decentralized agro-hydraulic polities exploiting even slight relief for intensive field systems. This finding may push back the chronology of terra preta creation in uplands and broaden the geographic scope of engineered Amazonian landscapes.
