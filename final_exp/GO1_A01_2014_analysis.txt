Context and Interpretation  
1. Regional Background  
   • The Itapirapuã interfluve (5CVV+3C) lies on a broad upland plateau about 10–20 m above the adjacent floodplain of the Paranã River. Previous surveys here have documented geoglyphs and raised‐field complexes, primarily on oxbow remnants.  
   • Historical occupation by complex pre‐Columbian societies (ca. AD 1000–1500) exploited nutrient‐poor soils through engineered water‐management earthworks such as canals, ditches, and raised platforms (“camellones”).  

2. How These Anomalies Fit the Broader Picture  
   • Anomaly 1’s rectilinear raised platforms closely parallel analogues at the Alto Alegre site (~200 km SE), where multiple aligned camellones served as flood‐water filters and cultivation plots.  
   • Anomaly 2’s linear canal may represent a feeder to these raised fields, hinting at a more extensive distribution network than previously appreciated in this part of Goiás.  

3. Implications for Archaeological Theory  
   • Challenges the assumption that intensive upland earthworks in Central Amazonian interfluves are restricted to river‐adjacent levees.  
   • Supports emerging models of regional integration via engineered drainage grids across terra-firme plateaus, implying higher population densities and landscape planning.
