Background and Interpretation  
1. Geographic & Historical Context  
   - The Nazaré Paulista interfluve lies at the northern edge of the Serra do Mar escarpment, where pre‐Columbian societies exploited subtle topographic gradients to manage water in well‐drained uplands.  
   - Ethnohistorical and soil‐chemical studies in nearby Pará and Tocantins have documented extensive raised‐field and canal systems, but southeastern São Paulo has been understudied.  

2. Regional Fit & Theoretical Implications  
   - The linear embankment (anomaly_1) and associated canal suggest a water‐retention scheme very similar to those found in the Amazonian Llanos (e.g., Bacajá) but relocated onto steeper upland terrain.  
   - The raised platforms (anomaly_2) may represent small garden plots or fishpond terraces, challenging the paradigm that Terra Preta–associated raised fields are limited to seasonally flooded lowlands.  
   - These anomalies imply sophisticated hydraulic engineering by pre‐Columbian groups in the highlands, potentially predating or contemporaneous with Guaraní expansions.  

3. Advancing Archaeological Theory  
   - Demonstrates that upland water‐management was not solely a floodplain adaptation, broadening the scope of Amazonian landscape archaeology.  
   - Supports a model of region‐wide exchange of soil‐enrichment practices (Terra Preta) and hydrological know-how, indicating cultural connections across interfluves.
