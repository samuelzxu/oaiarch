DISCUSSION  
1. Regional Context  
   • Paragominas sits on an interfluve between the Capim and Gurupi rivers, an area recently shown to host scattered geoglyph clusters and water‐management earthworks dating to ca. AD 1000–1400.  
   • Most known systems lie closer to river margins; these upland features would extend the settlement and engineering footprint farther into terra firme than previously documented.  

2. Historical Interpretation  
   • Small orthogonal ditches and embankments suggest a community-scale irrigation or drainage system, comparable in layout to those around the Upper Xingu but on a reduced scale.  
   • The rectangular enclosure (anomaly_1) could mark a domestic or communal plaza bounded by water channels, hinting at social organization around water control in uplands.  

3. Theoretical Implications  
   • Challenges the model that Amazonian water‐management was river‐centric. These interfluve installations indicate pre‐Columbian populations engineered even slight relief to optimize soil moisture and microtopography.  
   • Supports a more widespread, decentralized pattern of earth‐engineered upland villages, which may have been connected by causeways and minor canals.
