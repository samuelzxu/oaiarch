1. Geographic & historical context  
   • The interfluvial forests of Acre lie between the Juruá and Purus rivers, a region recently shown to host diverse pre‐Columbian earth‐management systems beyond the well‐known Amazonian floodplain.  
   • Previous surveys (e.g. Hilbert et al. 2020) documented geoglyphs and terra preta on major floodplains; upland field systems have been hypothesized but rarely mapped under closed canopy.  
   • Tupi‐Guarani groups and earlier unnamed farming cultures practiced raised‐field and canal constructions to manage seasonal water and soil fertility—these features extend that tradition onto higher terraces.  

2. Implications for theory  
   • Demonstrates that engineered raised‐field networks were not confined to floodplains but penetrated terra firme interfluves, challenging models that restrict Amazonian intensive agriculture to seasonally inundated soils.  
   • Suggests a more complex, regionally adapted agrarian economy—upland cultivation platforms and ditches could support year‐round cultivation in otherwise well-drained soils.  
   • May revise carry capacity estimates for pre‐Columbian populations and force re‐integration of upland terra preta and field systems into Amazonian “garden” models.
