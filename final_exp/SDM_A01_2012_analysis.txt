Context and Interpretation  
The Ubatuba interfluvial uplands lie within the Atlantic Forest biome, a region where extensive Amazon‐style raised fields and causeway systems have rarely been documented. Pre‐colonial Tupi‐Guarani and related groups in coastal São Paulo are known primarily for small horticultural clearings rather than large engineered earthworks. The discovery of parallel ridges and a rectilinear platform here suggests either:  
- An undocumented local adaptation of Amazonian water‐management techniques at higher elevations, or  
- A localized experiment in soil amendment and moisture control for dry‐season cultivation.  

These features would push current models of south‐eastern Amazonian cultural influence beyond the floodplain, indicating exchange networks or population movements into the Serra do Mar escarpment. If confirmed by excavation or geophysics, they would challenge the notion that large‐scale earthen infrastructure was confined to lowland várzea zones.
