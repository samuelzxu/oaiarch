IV. Reporting  

The two anomalies fall at ~240 m a.s.l. on a gentle plateau between tributaries of the Rio Branco‐Xingu interfluve. Pre‐Columbian societies in Mato Grosso are known for raised‐field agriculture and canal networks (ca. AD 800–1400).  
- Anomaly 1 (linear depression) may represent a feeder canal or drainage ditch linking upland fields to minor streams.  
- Anomaly 2 (orthogonal corner) resembles the corner of a small enclosure or raised‐field parcel, echoing patterns documented further east in Rondônia.  

These finds extend the known agricultural frontier westward by ~50 km and suggest that smallholder water‐management systems were more widespread in interfluvial forest zones than current models predict. If confirmed by ground‐truthing, this would support hypotheses of decentralized, multi‐node farmsteads rather than large nucleated settlements in this sector.
