Region & Historical Context  
This portion of the Vila do Japim interfluve lies between the Japim and Araras igarapés, an area recently recognized for scattered earthworks tied to late‐Holocene agro‐hydraulic systems. Previous survey work noted similar embankments and canal pairs near seasonally inundated lowlands, but never this deep into the forested plateau. The detected features echo the profile‐and‐plan curvature anomalies recorded by Heckenberger et al. (2008) in the Upper Xingu, suggesting extension of that tradition into Rondônia.

Broader Implications  
– These embankment–ditch pairs, if confirmed by ground inspection, indicate that intensive water‐management was practiced not only along floodplains but well within interfluves, demanding a reevaluation of settlement distribution models in southwestern Amazonia.  
– The curvilinear causeway implies community‐level coordination in channel routing, challenging the assumption that compact, circular plaza villages dominated this sector. Instead, we may be looking at a semi‐hierarchical network of hamlets linked by engineered watercourses.  

Challenges to Current Theory  
– Conventional wisdom holds that “terra firme” above levee heights saw minimal earthwork construction. These anomalies push the boundary upslope, indicating designers exploited subtle micro‐topography for hydrological control far from riverbanks.  
– Models of social complexity in the region emphasize large plazas; these narrow, linear systems suggest an alternative social organization focused on resource‐exchange corridors rather than centralized ceremonial cores.
