1. Regional and Historical Context  
The Feliz Natal interfluve sits between tributaries of the Juruena–Teles Pires system, an upland area with well‐drained soils but pronounced seasonal rainfall. Ethnohistoric and archaeological surveys have documented dense networks of raised fields, causeways, and canals radiating from known sites such as Serrinha do Barro and Feliz Natal itself, attributed to Late Holocene complex agrarian communities (sometimes linked to a local variant of the Tapajós cultural sphere).

2. How These Anomalies Fit Broader Patterns  
The parallel ridges (anomaly_1) match canonical raised‐field dimensions (ridge width ~5 m, inter‐ridge spacing ~20–30 m) documented at Rancho do Tatu and Lago do Limão. The sinuous ditch (anomaly_2) resembles feeder canals described by Erickson (2000) in Bolivian Amazonia, suggesting an integrated water‐capture network feeding adjacent raised fields. Their orientation upslope of minor drainage lines confirms past strategies of microcatchment management.

3. Theoretical Implications  
These features extend the known distribution of engineered upland fields deeper into the Mato Grosso interior, challenging models that confine large‐scale water management to floodplain margins. They suggest more extensive planning and labor coordination than previously assumed for this locale, supporting theories of pan‐Amazonian complexity and regional political economies well before European contact.
