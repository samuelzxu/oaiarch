Region & Historical Context  
The Belterra interfluve lies between the Tapajós and Xingu basins and hosts a network of geoglyphs, raised fields and canals dating to ca. AD 500–1250. These earthworks were once thought confined to seasonally flooded várzea, but recent LiDAR work suggests intentional water‐management on terra firme uplands. The rectangular enclosures and L-shaped channels we observe here align with patterns documented near Monte Alegre and the Santarém region, indicating a much broader spatial extent. This challenges the prevailing model that upland Amazonia lacked intensive landscape engineering, reinforcing theories of complex, regionwide agro‐hydraulic systems.
