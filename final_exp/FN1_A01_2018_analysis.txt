Region and Historical Context  
The Feliz Natal area in Mato Grosso sits within an interfluvial landscape historically assumed to be of low pre‐Columbian landscape engineering intensity. However, recent LiDAR surveys across adjacent regions (e.g., along the Xingu headwaters) have documented networks of raised fields, canals, and drainage ditches beyond traditional floodplain settlements. The two anomalies identified here extend that pattern farther west, suggesting local groups exploited subtle upland plateaus for horticulture, draining seasonally saturated soils with canal–ridge complexes.

Implications for Archaeological Theory  
1. Extends the known western limit of upland water‐management systems in southern Amazonia.  
2. Challenges the notion that intensive earthworks were confined to riverine floodplains; small‐scale forest‐cleared plateaus may have supported productive raised fields.  
3. Suggests a mosaic model of pre‐Columbian land use where both lowland and upland engineered fields coexisted, linking agricultural corridors across interfluves.
