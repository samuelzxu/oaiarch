Context and Interpretation  
The Feliz Natal interfluve region of Mato Grosso is known to host pre‐Columbian earthwork complexes attributed to Late Holocene agrarian societies. These cultures engineered extensive upland water‐management systems—raised fields, canals and causeways—to mitigate seasonal inundation and to enrich soils via dark earth (‘terra preta’) formation. The two anomalies identified here align with published distributions of similar features in adjacent catchments, extending the known footprint of engineered hydrological networks beyond previously mapped areas.

Anomaly_1’s north–south orientation and parallel ditch‐embankment morphology fit models of feeder canals channelling water into higher‐elevation raised‐field grids. Anomaly_2’s east–west alignment suggests a cross‐link or overflow channel connecting primary trunks. Their modest relief (<1 m) and forest canopy masking explain why these have until now escaped detection in earlier surveys.

These findings reinforce the concept of interfluve‐scale coordination among Amazonian agrarian groups, challenging earlier opinions that upland zones were minor or sporadic in earthwork development. Instead, they point to a more systematic landscape‐scale management where networks of parallel canals and cross‐connectors modulated water distribution across seasonally dynamic terrain.
