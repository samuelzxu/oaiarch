Reporting:

The Bento Manoel Rodrigues sector of the Oriximiná interfluve lies at the nexus of pre-Columbian exchange routes along the Rio Trombetas tributaries. Previous surveys have documented dispersed earthworks—raised fields and fish-weirs—along valley bottoms, but evidence for upland canal systems has been scarce. 

1. Regional and historical context  
   - The plateau between the Trombetas and Cupari rivers supported dense settlements from ca. AD 1000–1600, with robust agro-hydraulic engineering.  
   - Published work (Erickson et al. 2021; Heckenberger 2003) describes networks of canals feeding raised fields; this find suggests those networks extended farther into interfluve plateaus than assumed.  

2. Fit with broader scholarship  
   - The NE–SW causeway complements known valley bottom canals, implying intentional routing of runoff from uplands into fish-ponds.  
   - The adjacent mounded platforms align with domestic clusters observed at Monte Alegre and Curuá, supporting a pattern of clustered settlements around water-control features.  

3. Theoretical implications  
   - Challenges the model of strictly valley-centric earthworks by showing upland channels integrated into Amazonian hydraulic landscapes.  
   - Suggests greater territorial planning and inter-site connectivity across upland interfluves, refining our understanding of Amazonian sociopolitical complexity.  

4. Actionable insights
