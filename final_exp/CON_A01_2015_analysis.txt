Discussion and Context

The rectilinear geometry and consistent orientation of anomaly_1 strongly resemble small‐scale field drains reported in the upper Xingu and Tapajós basins, but their occurrence on steeper upland terrain (~5°) is novel. These discoveries challenge prevailing models that Amazonian water‐management was confined to floodplain settings. They suggest complex, upland hydraulic networks that conveyed water to slightly convex plateaus or retentive soils. Incorporating such features into regional hydrological reconstructions may revise estimates of pre‐contact land use intensity and forest clearance.  

By situating these ditches within broader settlement patterns—likely aligned with hidden raised‐field platforms yet to be mapped—this analysis advances the theory that Amazonian interfluves hosted integrated field systems rather than isolated garden plots. Further ground‐truthing and soil coring at these coordinates could yield charcoal and pottery datable to Late Holocene ceramic phases.
