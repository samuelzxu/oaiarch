Background & Interpretation  
1. Geographic & Historical Context  
   • The Glória interfluve (69V4+H3) lies on a low‐relief plateau between the Acre and Mâncio Lima rivers. Archaeological surveys in nearby zones have documented pre‐Columbian agro‐hydraulic systems dated ca. 1200–1500 CE.  
   • The identified canal–embankment networks and raised‐field clusters align with known terra preta settlements in southwestern Amazonia, suggesting coordinated water management for seasonal flooding and soil enrichment.

2. How These Anomalies Advance Current Models  
   • The sinuous canal (anomaly_1) is longer and more regular than canals previously recorded in Acre, implying a larger, perhaps regional, irrigation scheme.  
   • The platform cluster (anomaly_2) indicates modular raised‐field agriculture beyond the small hamlets earlier described here, extending the known footprint of staple–crop production.  
   • The causeway (anomaly_3) may represent a trade or communication route linking floodplain villages, challenging the notion that upland interfluves were isolated pockets.

3. Implications for Amazonian Archaeology  
   • These features support a paradigm of high‐density, landscape‐scale engineering by late Holocene societies in western Amazonia.  
   • The integrated use of LiDAR derivatives and spectral anomalies corroborates multi‐functional design: canals for drainage, raised fields for cultivation, and causeways for transport.
