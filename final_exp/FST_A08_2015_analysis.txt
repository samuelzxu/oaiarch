Regional Context and Significance  
The Oriximiná interfluve is known for dispersed forest villages and complex raised‐field systems dating to the Late Holocene. The newly detected embankment–depression system near 8P76+JQ Almeida expands the mapped extent of water‐management infrastructure in this sector. Previously, most documented canals lay closer to major rivers; this off‐channel feature suggests inland settlement and agriculture was more intensive than assumed. The regular geometry of the embankment’s angular turns aligns with other Amazonian causeways, while the adjacent depressions may mark domestic or cultivation units.

Implications for Theory  
Current models posit that dense pre‐Columbian populations clustered along floodplains. These anomalies challenge that view by demonstrating substantial earthworks in true interfluve forests. It implies a wider territorial reach of landscape engineering and suggests that regional polities orchestrated large‐scale drainage and irrigation networks beyond river margins. This pushes us to reconsider the socio‐political complexity and ecological footprint of upland Amazonian societies.
