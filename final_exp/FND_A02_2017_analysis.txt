Contextual Integration  
The MT-225 sector of Feliz Natal sits on an upland interfluve between the Teles Pires and Juruena tributaries. Pre-Columbian populations here engineered extensive hydraulic landscapes—canals, causeways and raised fields—to manage seasonal water surplus and nutrient‐rich black earth (‘terra preta’). Anomaly 1 fits into the broader network of NE–SW oriented causeway–canal complexes documented less than 10 km to the east, while Anomaly 2 echoes raised‐field clusters found in the adjacent MT-224 survey area.

The orientation and spacing of the canal–causeway complex (Anomaly 1) match regional patterns of upland water control used to shuttle water off plateau tops into adjacent valleys. The raised-field ensemble (Anomaly 2) amplifies known distribution of agricultural cells, extending cultivation zones well into areas formerly considered too drained for intensive horticulture.

Theoretical Implications  
1. Upland water‐management was more pervasive than models have assumed—these features lie well above any active floodplain and show deliberate engineering of micro-catchments on gentle slopes (<2°).  
2. The co-occurrence of canal complexes with terra preta indicators in densely forested interfluves suggests a tighter integration of transport corridors and soil‐amendment zones than current ﬂoodplain-centric paradigms allow.  
3. These discoveries push the known upper‐limits of Amazonian hydraulic networks into plateau core areas, calling for revised population and land-use density estimates in upland sectors.
